Two layers

We love nature, and we can control it. That sentence is the whole identity. Everything meridian shows the world is a natural surface — raked sand, still water, moss on bark, a dried leaf — photographed warm and calm, japandi, one product in it.

Under the hood is the lab. Beneath every surface sits the same image rendered as data: characters, dots, a grid. It appears on hover, on scroll, inside the pack, in the loading state. It is never the first thing you see and it is always there.

Surface is what we sell. Under the hood is why you can trust it.

Wordmark

meridian, lowercase, always. One serif face, one weight, set tight, in forest green. Nothing sits beside it and nothing sits behind it.

The M badge is the mark. A capital M, reversed out of a small rounded square in forest green. It lives small and away from the wordmark: bottom right of the label, the app icon, the favicon, the cap. On the cube it is the same M, pressed rather than printed.

Clear space on every side is the width of the m. Ink on vanilla and paper; vanilla on ink and bark. Never on the Ecophilia green — on that ground the product carries the name.

Colour

Six natural tones and one loud one. Vanilla is the ground for almost everything. Sand and caramel are what vanilla does in warm light. Moss and bark are what sits on it. Forest is the ink: there is no black anywhere, the type on the label and on the page is this green.

Ecophilia green is the exception. It is the colour of the product book and of the layer under the hood, and it belongs nowhere else. It never becomes a background for the wordmark and never tints a photograph.

Every value here was read off the photography, not picked from a wheel.

Type

Instrument Serif is the voice. It is the wordmark and every headline, always lowercase at display sizes, set tight, never bold and never italic for emphasis.

Inter does the reading: body, labels, the compound name on the pack, dosage, navigation. Regular and medium only.

JetBrains Mono is under the hood. Batch numbers, lab data, the ASCII layer, captions in this book. It is set in signal green when it sits on vanilla and in vanilla when it sits on ink. It never carries a headline.

Photography

A real surface, warm low light, one product. The ground is always a natural material that has been touched by a hand: sand that has been raked, water that has been disturbed once, a branch that has been cut. That is the whole brand in one picture — nature, and the evidence that we shaped it.

Light is late afternoon, from the side, so the surface has grain and the product has a shadow. Palette stays in the six natural tones. Nothing candid, no people, no interiors.

The product is never treated: the label reads, the cap is vanilla, nothing crosses the glass.

Ecophilia

The product book. Constantin Boym’s Ecophilia puts one found branch on one flat green page and lets it be the whole page. We take the format and subvert the subject: every page is a new offering, sitting on or hanging off a different piece of nature.

One product per page. One natural object per page, and never the same kind twice: lichen, bark, a cut branch, a leaf, a stone. The green is flat and exact. The type, when it comes, is Helvetica-plain and small, top right, the way the book does it.

This is the only place the Ecophilia green lives, and the only place the product is shown on a coloured ground.

Under the hood

The same picture, as data. Every surface photograph has a twin: the frame rendered in monospace characters, signal green on vanilla, dense where the image is dark, empty where it is light. It is generated from the photograph, never drawn.

Where it shows. On hover, the surface gives way to the hood for as long as the cursor stays. On scroll it runs under the hero. Inside the pack it is printed on the inner face of the box. In the app it is the loading state and the lab-results view.

Where it never shows. On the product, on the label, as a background for the wordmark, or as decoration on its own. It only ever appears as the underside of a real photograph.

Packaging

Not final. This is the system it will follow. Two formats. The vial: clear glass, a vanilla cap over a silver band, a white paper label. The cube: a warm vanilla chewable with the m pressed into its face, no print on it at all.

The label. Everything in forest green on warm paper. The wordmark first, then a hairline rule. The product name in bold sans — M-3 RT — with the dose beneath it, then a second rule, then 99% Purity and Research Use Only at the foot, and the M badge bottom right. No colour beyond the green, no pattern — the hood is printed inside the box, not on the label.

The cap is the one place colour lives on the pack, and it is vanilla on every product. Offerings are told apart by the name, not by a colour code.
